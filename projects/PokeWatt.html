OVERVIEW

PokeWatt is a side-scrolling Pokémon game with no controller. The controller is the power meter on my bike trainer, and the buttons are my legs. Ride easy and the world ambles past, mostly populated by the Pokémon equivalent of pigeons. Ride hard and rarer Pokémon turn up to see what all the fuss is about.

HOW IT PLAYS

Three numbers from the pedals run the whole show:

  • Speed sets how fast the scenery scrolls and where you sit on screen (further left the faster you go). Don't stop pedaling.
  • Cadence drives the pedaling animation and how often the game rolls the dice for a new Pokémon: about every 5 seconds when you're barely turning the cranks, down to about every second at 120 rpm.
  • Power decides which kind of Pokémon you meet, measured against your FTP (the most power you can hold for about an hour, a number cyclists bring up at parties).

Okay, but which Pokémon?

Power picks the tier; speed picks the Pokémon. Inside the tier, every candidate is ranked by how closely its base Speed stat matches how fast you're riding, and the winner is drawn with a bias toward the closest match. So crawling along tends to attract Slowpoke, and a sprint brings out Regeleki.

Once a Pokémon turns up it drifts around you for 5–10 seconds and then leaves. If it was a poor match for how you're riding for two seconds straight, it gets bored and leaves early. Each one also has a 1-in-100 chance of being shiny, which is exactly as exciting as it is on the handheld.

THE BOSS

Every minute a boss walks into the scene. Right now that boss is Mewtwo, who is the only boss, and who has made peace with that. Your biking disturbs its sleep, so Metwo wakes up, and waits to see what you've got. Every second you hold at least 150% of your FTP (262 W for a 175 W FTP) costs it one of its ten health points. Drain them all and it faints, and the next boss shows up a minute later. While it's out, wild Pokémon flee to the right in a panic and no new ones spawn, because it's a one-on-one situation now.

FAIR WARNING Hits count total seconds above the line, not consecutive ones, and there is no penalty for dipping below it. A boss that doesn't punish you for resting is generous. A boss that doesn't punish you for anything is a work in progress.

DAY AND NIGHT

The scenery follows your computer's real clock. Ride at noon and it's a sunny day with mountains and pine trees. Ride at 3 a.m. and there's a city across the water with its lights on, and you are the only person awake in it. The sky is one picture whose palette slides through day, sunset and night, the way the original handhelds did it, so there are no drawings fading into other drawings. UNDER THE HOOD

Not a lot of machinery, but each piece had a reason. Every card lists what it buys and what it gives up.

A fixed 800×480 canvas

Everything is drawn onto one small surface, then scaled to fit whatever window you have.

BUYS Every position and sprite size is tuned once and stays right at any window size.

COSTS Black bars when the window is the wrong shape.

main.py · scale_and_blit_game_surface

Bluetooth on its own thread

The power meter is read on a background thread, which hands the game a smoothed snapshot.

BUYS The game never waits on Bluetooth, and watts and cadence are averaged over three seconds so a single twitchy reading doesn't spawn Mew.

COSTS The numbers lag you by a moment. Your legs will know before the screen does.

powermeter.py · PowerMeterReader

Speed worked out from watts

Speed isn't measured. It's estimated from power, using the rule that power goes up with the cube of speed.

BUYS No speed sensor needed. One power meter is the whole setup.

COSTS It assumes a flat road with no wind and a typical rider. Hills and headwinds are, as far as the game knows, a rumor.

powermeter.py · estimate_speed_mph

How I checked it

There is no formal test suite, because this is a game I ride. Instead I wrote throwaway scripts that draw the scene headlessly at any hour, replay a whole day in under a minute, and flip scenes at random moments mid-transition to see whether anything breaks. The scripts live outside the repository, so you'll have to take my word for it, or ride it yourself and tell me.

KNOWN LIMITS

SPEED IS A GUESS It's estimated from watts on a flat, windless road. Real speed on real roads would be better, and would need a speed sensor.
ONE BOSS It's always Mewtwo. The shoot animation is only decoration.
SETTINGS LIVE IN THE CODE The meter's Bluetooth address, your FTP and the choice between live and replay data are edited in main.py. A config file is on the list.
ONE SETUP Developed on Windows 11 with Favero Assioma pedals. Other Bluetooth power meters that speak the standard cycling power service should work, but I haven't tried any.
SHY POKÉMON 59 of the 1,025 have no walking sprite yet, so the game skips them.

RUN IT

You need Python 3.9 or newer.

pip install -r requirements.txt
python main.py

Out of the box it replays a recorded ride, so you can watch the whole thing without any hardware. To ride for real, point it at your meter (python scan_devices.py finds the address) and set your FTP in main.py. The keyboard is for testing:
